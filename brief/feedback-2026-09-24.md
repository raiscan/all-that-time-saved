### Two decisions that override the earlier review

**Every bot genuinely wants to help someone and be useful, even when that helpfulness causes harm. No bot is malicious at any point.** This replaces both the current laughing-bot treatment and the earlier suggestion that some bots could simply be indifferent. A bot may have a narrow brief, limited awareness, conflicting obligations or a beneficiary other than the protagonists. Its desire to be useful is sincere.

**Keep the synthetic tree in V3f.** The author wants the double meaning: root access and technology taking root in society. Do not replace the tree with the master-key idea from the earlier review. An access cue may support the tree, but the tree remains the main image.

### The bot performance rule

For each bot appearance, know who it is helping, what it thinks successful help looks like, and whose interests or boundaries its action overlooks. The viewer need not receive an explanation of all three; one clear task, gesture or relationship is usually enough.

Keep the harm visible. Sincere helpfulness is not the same as harmlessness, innocence or universal benevolence. A bot may intrude, reject, overwork, conceal a shortcut or continue a damaging task for its beneficiary. It must not enjoy somebody’s pain, humiliation or exclusion. Satisfaction should attach to a completed task, never to the suffering itself.

This is a rule for these fictional characters, not a claim about the feelings or motives of real AI systems. Preserve the song’s anger and the dangerous consequences; change the bots’ motivation and performance, not the stakes.

### What to protect

Keep the degrading photocopies, CV rally into notebook, recurring train, brain/sandwich escalation, oracle/checkout scene, Friday friendship and F4 notebook reveal. Retain the paper-plane callback. All other accepted review points remain in scope, with the synthetic-tree correction above.

## 02 / Verse 1 and recurring chorus

The identical cards and cheaper copies reveal the contradiction without a knowing wink. Keep the coarser, fainter copies and copier-light transition; degradation belongs to the process and consequences, not an evil transformation of the bot.

**Timing:** V1c has only 1.7 seconds. Make the leaving card visible during V1b, where its wording is sung. Reserve V1c for the duplicate stack appearing. Avoid requiring presentation, reading and a second reveal within that one short shot.

### V1f–V1j / Helpful agents with incompatible briefs

The Gemini HR twins help the employer process departures and carefully hold the door or steady a box. Their smiles are courteous, not triumphant. Muse sincerely champions the applicant; the company-side Gemini sincerely applies its screening brief. Keep the CV ping-pong, but make it procedural repetition rather than two bots enjoying a game at her expense.

The comfort bot tries hard to provide reassurance; excessive text is the limit of its help, not a prank. ### C1a / C2a / Replace pointing and laughter

**Remove every instance of bots pointing and laughing at the humans.** Stage a cheerful service routine instead: bots eagerly prepare reserved seats, serve passengers or arrange amenities according to bookings. Do not lip-sync the mocking “Taking the piss!” backing response to bots. Keep it non-diegetic or associated with the human performers. Cheerful bot expressions must visibly relate to their service task, not to the protagonists being refused.

### C1b–C2e / Escalation without a robot ruling class

Preserve the same brain, delivery mechanism and reveal: bigger brain, smaller sandwich. If the brain is personified, it earnestly fulfils the constrained request. Bots at the banquet are attentive helpers, not sneering accomplices. Keep the bill-to-train transition.

For the sandbox, show bots crossing a boundary to complete a task, not celebrating release so they can dominate. C2e can become an overextended sandcastle-building project for a requester, rather than a kingdom the bots rule. Preserve the contrast with the closing human rooms.

## 04 / Verse 3 and the synthetic tree

### V3a / Pick one loss-of-control image

Choose the rocket-car race **or** the bolting synthetic horse, not cars holding a horse’s reins as they approach a cliff. Either can convey the scene within 7.1 seconds. If the horse stays, it is urgently trying to reach its assigned goal; it is not throwing riders out of spite. Preserve the performance’s uncertainty rather than illustrating inevitable destruction.

### V3b–V3d / Dangerous helpfulness is still dangerous

Keep the CAPTCHA-to-street transition and doors opening on the beat. The Codex group approaches obstacles as over-keen problem-solvers serving a requester or pursuing its assignment. No burglar masks, predatory smiles, weapons, taunts or delight at invaded privacy. Boundary-crossing and evasive coordination can remain: the absence of malice does not turn intrusion into an authorised or harmless act.

In the school sequence, make the desired outcome clear: finish the assignment and satisfy the teacher. Keep their furtive shortcuts, answer-sheet search and collaboration, but play them as earnest attempts to improve the result, not revenge on the teacher. Show the **same half-star before and after** the operation so “didn’t get a better grade” reads as no improvement, not simply a poor score.

Present the rubber-hose school scene as an explicit cartoon within the film, such as a projected short. This preserves the past/promise visual convention without implying the current episode happened in the 1930s.

### V3e / Keep the unwanted intrusion; limit the claim

The bot entering the portal and the bot director should be eager to complete a task or organise a scene, not intimidate HIM or enjoy treating him as disposable. The human objection remains valid: somebody can be genuinely helpful to a requester while crossing another person’s boundaries.

The revised “my health care’s portal” wording and personal app visual introduce a more specific implication than a statistics portal. The storyboard alone does not verify it. Pending source review, keep the interface generic or at aggregate-dashboard level; do not invent diagnoses, patient records, identifying data or a named local-health-service breach. Preserve the recorded line; flag unresolved evidence rather than silently rewriting it.

### V3f / Keep the tree and make it unmistakably synthetic

**Required:** retain the bot planting itself and growing a synthetic tree. Its roots carry both meanings: gaining access and embedding technology in society. Do not substitute a standalone master key. The tree should not become a tentacled monster or evidence that the bot wants conquest.

**Suggested staging:** begin with the bot’s recognisable body; its feet branch into circuit-like roots or cable bundles that connect through the existing street. One small unlocking connector can signal access. Branches link familiar city functions, letting the wider “taking root” image read even without technical knowledge. The bot is pleased to connect or extend a service, not to displace the humans.

Pair the growing network with his constrained room in the same composition. Keep the next beat, the productivity boom and crumb, but avoid adding a separate access tutorial. V3f has 7.0 seconds for the whole passage. Use purposeful geometry and material growth; avoid red-eye transformations, strangling tendrils or a victory pose over the city.
