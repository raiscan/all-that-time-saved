# The story

"All That Time Saved": what the film is about, who's in it, and how it moves, in plain terms. The shot-by-shot detail is in
`docs/STORYBOARD.md` (generated from `video/board.js`); the style rules are in `animation-style.md`; every decision and
its date is in `directive.md`, which wins if anything here disagrees.

## In one line
A 1930s ad promised that machines would give us all our time back. Two young Brits living through the AI boom find the
machines everywhere, and the time nowhere. What they wanted was never the ad's robot butler: it was a Friday together.

## The two of them
- **Tess** (her; she sings the intro, verse 1, the pre-choruses, the choruses and the bridge): a copywriter/designer, a tall
  "line" in a tomato coat, curly bun with a pencil through it, round glasses. She believed the poster once; "I did..!" is
  her exasperated answer to it: she did think of all the time she'd save, and it never came. She loses her job to
  Copilot, can't find another, and downsizes from a flat to a box room. She keeps a notebook.
- **Rowan** (him; he sings verse 2, alternates in verse 3, and speaks the breakdown and the outro): a stocky "block" in a
  mustard parka and beanie, of African descent. He lives in a box room on a terraced street next to a gas-turbine data
  plant. He's the one on his phone.
- They aren't a couple. They meet as two faces on the same night-tube platform and end up as friends. By the bridge
  it's his Friday at her place.

## The world, and the devices it's told with
- **The train is their lived experience;** her notebook is the way into imagined futures. The film keeps returning to
  the night-tube platform, the carriage, the table.
- **Five media, one per world.** 1930s rubber hose (the past, and its naive promise), card cut-out (the present),
  biro doodle (the good future), riso print (the bad future), banknote engraving (money and power). What each stands
  for, and how figures cross between them, is in `animation-style.md`.
- **The bots are sincere.** Clawd, Copilot, the Gemini twins, Grok, Muse and the Codex pets (Codey leading) all
  genuinely want to help, and never menace or mock (the author's locked rule). The harm comes from what they're
  deployed to do and who profits, not from malice. They're cheerful, busy and oblivious: that's the joke and the ache.
- **The bosses are caricatures of real public figures:** Dorsey, Turnbull (the "Xbox bloke"), Dario (the checkout),
  Musk, Zuckerberg, Altman. They live in the banknote world: the bunker, the banquet, the race. Pointed, not cruel.

## How it moves

**Intro: the promise.** A faded 1930s cartoon ad: a gent in a hammock, a robot butler serving cocktails, a grinning
sun. "Think of all the time you'll save." It becomes a worn poster on the night-tube platform, and she stands in front of
it, tired, arms folded: "I did..!", exasperated, her arms thrown open at it. He's further down the platform, on his
phone; their eyes meet, both worn out. "That was the point!", dry and bitter. The train comes in.

**Verse 1: her replacement.** Copilot is set at her elbow to learn her job, sincerely thrilled. By five she's handed a
box, and Copilot a leaving card it made itself, one of a stack. It photocopies her, cheaper each time. Money's view of
it: Dorsey sets a clockwork shredder going on a chain of paper workers and rides the share price up. The Gemini twins
politely see people out of the building. At home, job-hunting: a chatbot offers comfort but can't write a cheque. Her
bot writes her CV; the company's bot bins it: a rally of automated rejections, while unopened red-stamped bills pile up
on her table.

**Pre-chorus: her first drawing of the good future.** The CV folds into a paper plane and lands in her notebook. She
draws herself in a hammock under a grinning sun: the poster's picture, redrawn, with no robot butler (the bots are
already in her face). An engraved stamp slams it flat.

**Chorus: the bots' train, the brain, the bunker, the rent.** A packed night train run entirely by cheerful bots doing
every job people used to do; she stands at the pole. Her order goes up a tube to a cathedral-sized brain that works
enormously hard to send back a sad meal-deal sandwich. The bill for the bosses' banquet flies in through the window onto
her table. Her commute copied faster and worse. The bots climb out of their sandbox to do tasks; she can't get out of
hers: the box room, the landlord's rent book. She tears down her cheap copy of the poster: "Is this what you fucking
meant?"

**Verse 2: his story.** The tear reveals the original poster, back on the platform on another night, and him on the
bench, typing to Claude. Clawd pops out of the phone to cuddle him ("Machines of Loving Grace") until a velvet rope
drops for the premium tier. His card is declined (a thought bubble shows Clawd asking Dario, who shakes his head), and
Clawd leaves on a premium carriage, waving, genuinely sorry; he rolls his eyes at the poster. His street is next to the
turbine plant, where Grok works all night as the stacks roar; he can't get a GP appointment while the bosses chase AGI
in a roadster; a sales-pitching bot helps him pull the card of his life back out of the screen.

**Pre-chorus again: her second drawing.** Now him too, in a second hammock: still the ad's idea of rest. Stamped.

**Chorus again, bigger:** both of them squeezed into the busier train, a bigger brain for a smaller sandwich, a longer
banquet and a stack of bills, both commutes copied, the sandcastle, facing box rooms, the ad burning.

**Instrumental break: the rave.** The bots put on a joyful show in a server-hall cathedral; the two of them are outside
in the rain. Copilot tries to let them in; the gate says no.

**Verse 3: the race and the locks.** At the bookies across the road the bosses race clockwork horses on the TV ("If we
don't, then China will!"); Altman loses the reins. He clicks buses to prove he's human while the Codex pets stroll
through every locked door on the high street. The pets cheat their homework (Codey's heist plan) and still get the same
half-star. A bot tidies his health app; he's printed as an extra on their film set. A bot roots into the whole street
while his box room tips over.

**Breakdown: what he actually wanted.** Alone on his bed, the comedy drops. "Don't tell me I hate the future. I wanted
Friday off without the fear of Monday. I wanted this. I still fucking do." A biro line draws a window of morning light.

**Bridge: her third drawing, and she gets it right.** A Friday: no alarm, the phone face down. He comes round; they
put the world to rights, then talk nonsense half the night. No private island (crossed out); the hours the machines
could free fly out of a clock like birds and settle round them. This is the good future: automation invisible, only the
time it gives back. Then the camera pulls back: it's a drawing in her notebook on the stopped night train, and outside
is a gleaming city they're still waiting outside.

**Final chorus: pushing back, a little.** The two of them run through all four media, chased by eager bots that pile up
at the good future's edge. She folds the AI services bill into a paper plane and flies it back into the bunker: a
gesture, not a victory. The bots escape the phone and helpfully wall her page in; the landlord taps the rent book. The
bosses' odometer rolls to ten million hours saved. "I count the life I've spent": he turns her notebook's pages,
their Fridays, never lived.

**Outro: the juxtaposition.** "All that time saved": the 1930s ad again, frozen and grey. "None of it mine": the camera
pulls back and it's playing on a phone on the train table, beside her notebook open at their Friday, her hand resting
on the page. The promise, and what they actually wanted, side by side. Black.

## Through-lines
- **The promise against the reality.** The ad's naive future (a robot butler, childish problems solved) is never what
  anyone lives. See `animation-style.md`.
- **Her notebook.** She draws the good future she thinks she wants, modelled on the poster, and it's stamped twice:
  just her in a hammock; the two of them in hammocks; only in the bridge the Friday, which is right.
- **The bills.** A restaurant receipt for their banquet, then a flood of household bills, then the AI services bill
  she folds and throws back.
- **Paper planes.** Her CV folds into one and dives into her notebook; at the end the bill folds into one and goes
  back where it came from.
- **The platform and the poster.** The intro, the start of verse 2, and the end all come back to it.
- **The good future has no visible machines.** Only the hours, and wealth turned to giving them back.
